## 6 Periodicity in *T*

Exact periodicity is rare in the study of ζ. As a function of *t*, each summand $`n^{-it}=e^{-it\log n}`$ has the fixed frequency $`\log n`$. What grows with *t* is the cutoff and hence the largest active frequency, $`\log m=\tfrac12\log(t/(2\pi))+o(1)`$. The closest classical notions are Bohr’s almost periodicity in the half-plane $`\sigma>1`$ \[22, Ch. XI\] and the fixed coefficient functions $`C_k(p)`$ of the Riemann–Siegel expansion, which depend only on the fractional part $`p=\bigl\{\sqrt{t/2\pi}\bigr\}`$. The index *T* exposes structure of the second kind: nothing here is exactly periodic. The unnormalized weights decay with *T*, while their normalized profiles converge to a common unit-cell limit. This section studies that asymptotic repetition and attempts to explain its origin.

### 6.2 A continuous and periodic normalization

The near-repetition has an exact limiting shape. Figure [2](#fig-pinned-waves) overlays the six unit intervals of Figure [1](#fig-d1-periodicity) after two normalizations that put the waves on a common footing.

The first normalization rescales and centers: on $`[m,m+1]`$ the centered coordinate

```math
P(T)=T^{1/2}\Bigl(d_1(T)-\Phi\bigl(-1,\tfrac12,m+1\bigr)\Bigr),
\qquad
\Phi\bigl(-1,\tfrac12,m+1\bigr)=\sum_{k\ge m+1}\frac{(-1)^{k-m-1}}{\sqrt{k}},
```

undoes the $`T^{-1/2}`$ decay and measures *d₁* against the alternating sum of the remaining summand lengths (Φ is the Lerch transcendent).

The second normalization pins both ends of the cycle (which fall exactly at integer *T*) so they begin and end at zero: the endpoint values of *P* on $`[m,m+1]`$ are $`+\varepsilon_m`$ and $`-\varepsilon_{m+1}`$, where $`\varepsilon_n=n^{1/2}\bigl(\Phi(-1,\tfrac12,n)-d_1(n^{-})\bigr)`$.[^2] Subtracting the chord through them,

```math
\mathcal W(T)=P(T)-(1-x)\,\varepsilon_m+x\,\varepsilon_{m+1},
\qquad x=T-m,
```

pins each wave to zero at the two integer endpoints of its unit interval. Plotted against the fractional part $`x=\{T\}`$, the six waves nearly coincide, and as $`T\to\infty`$ they converge to the tangent waveform

<a id="eq-W-infty"></a>

```math
\mathcal W_{\infty}(x)
=-\tfrac12\tan(2\pi x)\,
\tan\!\bigl(2\pi(x-\tfrac14)(x-\tfrac34)\bigr),\qquad\text{(25)}
```

at rate $`O(1/T)`$; the convergence is proved in Appendix [A.6](a-proof-of-proposition-4-1-reality-and-positivity.md#sec-limits) (Corollary [A.6](a-proof-of-proposition-4-1-reality-and-positivity.md#cor-W-limit)).

### 6.3 Derivation of the tangent limit from Siegel’s $`F(\tau)`$

The tangent waveform [(25)](#eq-W-infty) is not an empirical fit: it can be derived from a function as old as Riemann’s unpublished notes. Evaluating the elementary integral in equation 5 of Siegel’s 1932 paper \[21\], Siegel arrives at a closed form he calls the “desired result”,

<a id="eq-siegel-F"></a>

```math
F(\tau)\;=\;
\frac{1}{1-e^{-2\pi i \tau}}
\;-\;
\frac{e^{i\pi \tau^2}}{e^{i\pi \tau}-e^{-i\pi \tau}} .\qquad\text{(26)}
```

For real τ its complex conjugate has the compact form

<a id="eq-siegel-Fbar"></a>

```math
\overline{F(\tau)}\;=\;\frac{e^{-i\pi\tau^2}-e^{-i\pi\tau}}{2i\sin(\pi\tau)}
\;=\;\frac{e^{-i\pi\tau^2}-e^{-i\pi\tau}}{e^{i\pi\tau}-e^{-i\pi\tau}},\qquad\text{(27)}
```

and it is the conjugate we work with below. The apparent poles at integer τ are removable: $`\tau^2-\tau`$ is even there, so the numerator of [(27)](#eq-siegel-Fbar) vanishes with the denominator, and $`\overline{F(\tau)}\to\tfrac12-n`$ as $`\tau\to n`$. Figure [4](#fig-F-curve) traces the curve.

<a id="fig-F-curve"></a>

<p align="center"><img src="../figures/fig_F_curve.png"></p>

**Figure 4:** The trace of $`\overline{F(\tau)}`$ of [(27)](#eq-siegel-Fbar) in the complex plane for $`-1.5\le\tau\le1.5`$, with red dots every $`0.1`$ in τ. The apparent poles at the integers are removable (black squares), with $`\overline{F(\tau)}\to\tfrac12-n`$ as $`\tau\to n`$: the values $`-\tfrac12`$, $`+\tfrac12`$, $`+\tfrac32`$ at $`n=1,0,-1`$. Points of the curve one apart in τ are exactly one unit apart in the plane: $`\overline{F(\tau+1)}-\overline{F(\tau)}=-e^{-i\pi(\tau^2+\tau)}`$, a unit vector; the gray chord joins $`\overline{F(0.18)}`$ to $`\overline{F(1.18)}`$ and has length exactly $`1`$. Generated by `fig_F_curve.py`.

<a id="rem-F-to-f"></a>


**Remark 6.1** (regarding *F* as the source of Siegel’s $`f(s)`$). *F* is more than an intermediate step in Siegel’s derivation: it is the source of his function *f*, the function that carries the first half of zeta, $`f(s)\approx\Sigma_1+R_{1ak}`$ (§[8.2](08-other-remainders.md#sec-kuznetsov) records the details as [(61)](08-other-remainders.md#eq-siegel-f)). In his §3 Siegel multiplies his equation 5, that is [(26)](#eq-siegel-F), by $`u^{-s}`$ and integrates along the bisector of the first quadrant, and under that operation the two terms of *F* separate into the two halves of zeta. The pole term $`1/(1-e^{-2\pi iu})`$ yields $`-(2\pi)^{s-1}e^{\,i\pi(1-s)/2}\,\Gamma(1-s)\,\zeta(1-s)`$, while the Gaussian term yields *f* itself:

<a id="eq-f-from-F"></a>

```math
f(s)\;=\;\bigl(e^{\pi i s}-1\bigr)
\int_0^{e^{i\pi/4}\infty}
\frac{u^{-s}\,e^{\pi i u^2}}{e^{\pi i u}-e^{-\pi i u}}\,\mathrm{d}u
\qquad (\sigma<0),\qquad\text{(28)}
```

the restriction $`\sigma<0`$ being needed only for convergence at the endpoint $`u=0`$; *f* is continued analytically from there, and the contour form [(62)](08-other-remainders.md#eq-siegel-58) of §[8.2](08-other-remainders.md#sec-kuznetsov) converges for every *s*. The same separation can be read off [(27)](#eq-siegel-Fbar): since $`1/(1-e^{2\pi i\tau})=-e^{-\pi i\tau}/(2i\sin\pi\tau)`$, the numerator term $`e^{-\pi i\tau^2}`$ of $`\overline{F}`$ is the $`f(s)`$ side and $`-e^{-\pi i\tau}`$ is the $`\zeta(1-s)`$ side, so the two lobes of Figure [4](#fig-F-curve) carry the two halves in their numerator. So *F* is the common source of both $`\Sigma_1+R_{1ak}`$ and its reflected counterpart (the yin-yang connection is drawn in §[6.4](#sec-yinyang-inf)), and this common origin is one route into the convergence proved by other means in Theorem [6.5](#thm-yin-convergence).


Everything we need is contained in the polar form of $`\overline F`$. Factoring the mean exponent $`e^{-i\pi(\tau^2+\tau)/2}`$ out of the numerator turns the difference of exponentials into a sine:

<a id="eq-F-polar"></a>

```math
\overline{F(\tau)}
=-\,\frac{\sin\!\bigl(\tfrac{\pi}{2}(\tau^2-\tau)\bigr)}{\sin(\pi\tau)}\;
e^{-\tfrac{i\pi}{2}(\tau^2+\tau)} .\qquad\text{(29)}
```

Now rescale. Let $`a=\sqrt{t/2\pi}`$, $`N=\lfloor a\rfloor`$, and $`\hat p=a-N`$ be the classical Riemann–Siegel quantities (the same ones used throughout Appendix [A](a-proof-of-proposition-4-1-reality-and-positivity.md#sec-positivity)), and substitute $`\tau=2\hat p-\tfrac12`$ into [(29)](#eq-F-polar). The three pieces collapse: $`\sin(\pi\tau)=-\cos(2\pi\hat p)`$, $`\tfrac{\pi}{2}(\tau^2-\tau)=2\pi\bigl(\hat p^2-\hat p-\tfrac1{16}\bigr)
+\tfrac{\pi}{2}`$, and $`\tfrac{\pi}{2}(\tau^2+\tau)=2\pi\hat p^2-\tfrac{\pi}{8}`$, so that

<a id="eq-F-C0"></a>

```math
\overline{F}\bigl(2\hat p-\tfrac12\bigr)
= C_0(\hat p)\,e^{-iA},
\qquad
A=2\pi\hat p^2-\tfrac\pi8,
\qquad
C_0(\hat p)=\frac{\cos\!\bigl(2\pi(\hat p^2-\hat p-\tfrac1{16})\bigr)}
{\cos(2\pi\hat p)} .\qquad\text{(30)}
```

In words: evaluated at $`\tau=2\hat p-\tfrac12`$, twice the fractional part less one half, $`\overline F`$ has amplitude exactly $`C_0(\hat p)`$, the leading coefficient of the Riemann–Siegel correction, and phase $`-A`$; both reappear, together with $`B=2\pi\hat p`$, in Lemma [A.1](a-proof-of-proposition-4-1-reality-and-positivity.md#lem-C0-trig).

The limit now assembles from the critical-line machinery of Appendix [A](a-proof-of-proposition-4-1-reality-and-positivity.md#sec-positivity). On the line the exact reduction [(199)](a-proof-of-proposition-4-1-reality-and-positivity.md#eq-exact-line) reads $`d_1=r/(2\cos u)`$; Gabcke’s form of the Riemann–Siegel expansion [(201)](a-proof-of-proposition-4-1-reality-and-positivity.md#eq-gabcke) gives *r* the leading term $`(-1)^{N-1}a^{-1/2}\,C_0(\hat p)`$, the amplitude of [(30)](#eq-F-C0); and the phase expansion [(197)](a-proof-of-proposition-4-1-reality-and-positivity.md#eq-cosu) gives $`\cos u=(-1)^{m+1}\cos\beta+O(1/T)`$, where $`\beta=2\pi(x-\tfrac14)(x-\tfrac34)`$ and $`x=\{T\}`$. In each half of the unit interval the offset is $`\hat p=x\pm\tfrac12`$ [(196)](a-proof-of-proposition-4-1-reality-and-positivity.md#eq-cut-cases), and the sign factors combine as in Steps 3 and 4 of the proof of Theorem [A.2](a-proof-of-proposition-4-1-reality-and-positivity.md#thm-line-bound), so that

```math
\hat d_1=\sqrt{m+1}\,d_1
\;\longrightarrow\;
\frac{C_0(x+\tfrac12)}{2\cos\beta}
\quad(x<\tfrac12),
\qquad
\hat d_1
\;\longrightarrow\;
1-\frac{C_0(x-\tfrac12)}{2\cos\beta}
\quad(x>\tfrac12),
```

the extra $`1`$ in the second case being the newly included summand. Two lines of the product formula $`\cos(A\mp B)=\cos A\cos B\pm\sin A\sin B`$ turn both branches into one and the same expression,

```math
\hat d_1
\;\longrightarrow\;
\tfrac12\bigl(1-\tan(2\pi x)\tan\beta\bigr)
\;=\;\tfrac12+\mathcal W_{\infty}(x)
\;=:\;d(x),
```

the tangent waveform of [(25)](#eq-W-infty) lifted by $`\tfrac12`$.

Written in branch form, with *g* recording the $`x<\tfrac12`$ branch,

<a id="eq-d-profile"></a>

```math
d(x)=
\begin{cases}
g(x), & x<\tfrac12,\\[2pt]
1-g(1-x), & x\ge\tfrac12,
\end{cases}
\qquad
g(y)=\frac{-\cos\bigl(2\pi y^2-\tfrac{5\pi}{8}\bigr)}
{2\cos(2\pi y)\,\cos\bigl(2\pi y(1-y)-\tfrac{3\pi}{8}\bigr)}.\qquad\text{(31)}
```

In [(31)](#eq-d-profile) the point $`y=\tfrac14`$ is a removable $`0/0`$ singularity of *g* (the numerator vanishes together with $`\cos(2\pi y)`$), filled in by continuity with $`g(\tfrac14)=\tfrac14`$; this supplies the values of *d* at $`x=\tfrac14`$ and, through the second branch, at $`x=\tfrac34`$. The $`d(x)`$ limit profile passes through $`(\tfrac14,\tfrac14)`$, $`(\tfrac12,\tfrac12)`$, $`(\tfrac34,\tfrac34)`$ and stays inside the band $`[m_0,\,1-m_0]`$ with $`m_0=0.2268951\ldots`$ (at finite *T* the excursions are slightly larger but still bounded between $`\tfrac15`$ and $`\tfrac45`$; Theorem [A.7](a-proof-of-proposition-4-1-reality-and-positivity.md#thm-d1-bounds) in Appendix [A.7](a-proof-of-proposition-4-1-reality-and-positivity.md#sec-d1-bounds)); in particular the fractional amount never approaches either end of its summand.

Figure [3](#fig-d-limit) plots the $`d(x)`$ limit profile against the exact normalized weight $`\hat d_1=\sqrt{m+1}\,d_1`$: the convergence is at rate $`O(1/T)`$, and the samples at $`T=10+x`$ already sit on the curve. Theorem [A.5](a-proof-of-proposition-4-1-reality-and-positivity.md#thm-d1-limit) in Appendix [A.6](a-proof-of-proposition-4-1-reality-and-positivity.md#sec-limits) makes the derivation of this subsection rigorous, controlling every error term including those at the transition points $`x=0,\tfrac12,1`$.

### 6.4 Separating $`\overline F`$ into two curves: yin and yang at infinity

Because the curve in Figure [4](#fig-F-curve) resembles a *taijitu* (<img src="../figures/taijitu.png" height="15">), we call its two component curves yin and yang. The pair of curves turns the limit profile [(31)](#eq-d-profile) into a geometric picture. Write $`x=\{T\}`$ for the fractional part, so that *x* runs from $`0`$ to $`1`$ across each unit interval of *T*, and write

<a id="eq-psi-fn"></a>

```math
\Psi(x)\;:=\;
\frac{\cos\!\bigl(2\pi(x^2-x-\tfrac1{16})\bigr)}{\cos(2\pi x)}\qquad\text{(32)}
```

for the Riemann–Siegel correction function: it is the same function as *C₀* of [(30)](#eq-F-C0) (we keep Gabcke’s symbol *C₀* for the analytic input and Ψ for the limit curve), and it appears in §15.3 of Titchmarsh’s book \[22\] and in §5.4.1 of Pugh’s thesis \[20\]. Define the two curves

<a id="eq-yin-inf"></a>

```math
\mathrm{Yin}_{\infty}(x)\;:=\;
\Psi(x)\Bigl(-\cos\bigl(2\pi(x^2-\tfrac1{16})\bigr)
+i\sin\bigl(2\pi(x^2-\tfrac1{16})\bigr)\Bigr)+1
\;=\;1-\Psi(x)\,e^{-2\pi i(x^2-\frac1{16})}\qquad\text{(33)}
```

and

<a id="eq-yang-inf"></a>

```math
\mathrm{Yang}_{\infty}(x)\;:=\;
-e^{4\pi ix}\,\mathrm{Yin}_{\infty}(x)+e^{4\pi ix}
\;=\;e^{4\pi ix}\bigl(1-\mathrm{Yin}_{\infty}(x)\bigr).\qquad\text{(34)}
```

Both are built from $`\overline F`$ alone: the exponent $`2\pi(x^2-\tfrac1{16})`$ is the phase *A* of [(30)](#eq-F-C0), so $`\Psi(x)\,e^{-iA}=\overline F(2x-\tfrac12)`$ and

<a id="eq-yinyang-from-F"></a>

```math
\mathrm{Yin}_{\infty}(x)=1-\overline F\bigl(2x-\tfrac12\bigr),
\qquad
\mathrm{Yang}_{\infty}(x)=e^{4\pi ix}\,\overline F\bigl(2x-\tfrac12\bigr).\qquad\text{(35)}
```

As *x* runs over the unit interval the argument $`\tau=2x-\tfrac12`$ runs from $`-\tfrac12`$ to $`\tfrac32`$, from one lobe-meeting point of Figure [4](#fig-F-curve) to the other, covering both lobes once. The yin curve is the point reflection of that arc through $`\tfrac12`$; the yang curve is the same arc spun by the unit factor $`e^{4\pi ix}`$, two full turns per unit interval. Figure [5](#fig-yinyang-inf) draws the pair.

<a id="fig-yinyang-inf"></a>

<p align="center"><img src="../figures/fig_yinyang_infinity.png"></p>

**Figure 5:** The curves $`\mathrm{Yin}_{\infty}(x)`$ (blue, dashed) and $`\mathrm{Yang}_{\infty}(x)`$ (dark red, dashed) for $`0<x<1`$, with dots every $`0.1`$ in *x*; both are the single curve $`\overline F`$ of Figure [4](#fig-F-curve) repackaged as in [(35)](#eq-yinyang-from-F). The gray chord joins the two points at $`x=0.18`$; it crosses the real axis at $`d(0.18)=\tfrac12+\mathcal W_{\infty}(0.18)\approx0.228`$ (black dot), as Proposition [6.2](#prop-yinyang-chord) asserts for every *x*. Generated by `fig_yinyang_infinity.py`.

The pair of curves is more than a repackaging: the chord between them crosses the real axis at exactly the limit profile $`d(x)`$.

<a id="prop-yinyang-chord"></a>


**Proposition 6.2** (the chord recovers the limit profile). *For $`x\in(0,1)`$ with $`x\neq\tfrac14,\tfrac34`$, the line through $`\mathrm{Yin}_{\infty}(x)`$ and $`\mathrm{Yang}_{\infty}(x)`$ meets the real axis at the single point*

```math
\tfrac12+\mathcal W_{\infty}(x)\;=\;d(x),
```

*the limit profile of [(31)](#eq-d-profile).*



*Proof.* Abbreviate $`A=2\pi x^2-\tfrac\pi8`$, $`B=2\pi x`$, and $`\Psi=\Psi(x)`$, so that $`\mathrm{Yin}_{\infty}=1-\Psi e^{-iA}`$ and $`\mathrm{Yang}_{\infty}=\Psi e^{i(2B-A)}`$. The imaginary parts are $`\Psi\sin A`$ and $`\Psi\sin(2B-A)`$, the real parts $`1-\Psi\cos A`$ and $`\Psi\cos(2B-A)`$. The line through points $`z_1,z_2`$ with $`\mathrm{Im}z_1\neq\mathrm{Im}z_2`$ crosses the real axis at

```math
x_0
=\frac{\mathrm{Re}z_1\mathrm{Im}z_2
-\mathrm{Re}z_2\mathrm{Im}z_1}
{\mathrm{Im}z_2-\mathrm{Im}z_1} .
```

Substituting, the denominator is $`\Psi\bigl(\sin(2B-A)-\sin A\bigr)=2\Psi\cos B\sin(B-A)`$, and the numerator is $`\Psi\bigl(\sin(2B-A)-\Psi\sin 2B\bigr)`$. Since $`\Psi=\cos(A-B)/\cos B`$ (the form of *C₀* used in Lemma [A.1](a-proof-of-proposition-4-1-reality-and-positivity.md#lem-C0-trig)) and $`2\sin B\cos(A-B)=\sin A+\sin(2B-A)`$, the numerator collapses to $`-\Psi\sin A`$, so

```math
x_0=\frac{-\sin A}{2\cos B\,\sin(B-A)} .
```

Now let $`\beta=2\pi(x-\tfrac14)(x-\tfrac34)`$ as in §[6.3](#sec-tangent-derivation). Then $`A-B=\beta-\tfrac\pi2`$, so $`\sin(B-A)=\cos\beta`$; and $`B+\beta=A+\tfrac\pi2`$, so $`-\sin A=\cos(B+\beta)`$. Therefore

```math
x_0
=\frac{\cos(B+\beta)}{2\cos B\cos\beta}
=\tfrac12\bigl(1-\tan(2\pi x)\tan\beta\bigr)
=\tfrac12+\mathcal W_{\infty}(x)
=d(x).
```

Since $`|\beta|\le\tfrac{3\pi}8<\tfrac\pi2`$ on $`[0,1]`$, the factor $`\cos\beta`$ never vanishes; the excluded points $`x=\tfrac14,\tfrac34`$ are exactly where $`\cos B=0`$, and there $`\sin A=\sin(2B-A)=0`$: both ends of the chord land on the real axis itself, and the crossing point is recovered instead by continuity, $`d(\tfrac14)=\tfrac14`$ and $`d(\tfrac34)=\tfrac34`$. ◻

### 6.5 General yin and yang curves

What are the yin and yang curves when σ is not $`\tfrac12`$ and *T* is not infinite? Nothing in their construction requires either limit. Return to the exact decomposition $`\zeta=\Sigma_1+\Sigma_2+R`$ of [(10)](03-reparameterization-and-cutoff-choice-the-i-t-map.md#eq-zeta-T) and, as in §[4](04-decomposing-the-remainder-r-r1-r2.md#sec-decomp), write $`t=I(T)`$, $`s=\sigma+it`$, and $`M=\lceil T\rceil`$. The natural frame in which to watch the remainder is the one that makes the next summand of the main sum the unit segment: translate the end of the main sum, Σ₁, to the origin, and divide by the summand vector $`M^{-s}`$, that is,

<a id="eq-frame-map"></a>

```math
z\;\longmapsto\;(z-\Sigma_1)\,M^s.\qquad\text{(36)}
```

In this coordinate frame, the next summand of the main sum runs from $`0`$ to $`1`$. Define two points in this frame,

<a id="eq-yin1"></a>

```math
\mathrm{Yin}(s)\;:=\;R\,M^s\qquad\text{(37)}
```

and

<a id="eq-yang1"></a>

```math
\mathrm{Yang}(s)\;:=\;\mathrm{Yin}(s)\;-\;\chi(s)\,M^{2s-1}.\qquad\text{(38)}
```

The first point is where the local coordinate frame map [(36)](#eq-frame-map) sends $`\Sigma_1+R=\zeta-\Sigma_2`$, the end of the remainder. The second point is where the transformation to the local frame sends $`\Sigma_1+R-\chi M^{s-1}`$, the same point after the dual sum takes one more step $`n=M`$: subtracting the next dual summand $`\chi M^{s-1}`$ and applying [(36)](#eq-frame-map) contributes the $`\chi M^{2s-1}`$ term. As *T* sweeps with σ fixed, the two points trace the general yin and yang curves.[^3]

The chord property of Proposition [6.2](#prop-yinyang-chord) is not special to the limit: it is the $`T\to\infty`$ limit of an identity that holds exactly at every finite *T*, which we state next. Figure [6](#fig-yinyang-general) draws the pair for $`1\leq T\leq2`$ on the critical line over the limit curves of §[6.4](#sec-yinyang-inf), with the chord at $`T=1.18`$.

<a id="prop-yinyang-chord-finite"></a>


**Proposition 6.3** (the chord recovers the fractional amount of the next main summand). *Fix σ and non-integer T with $`\sin(2\omega+\psi)\neq0`$, where $`\omega=t\log M`$ and $`\psi=\arg\chi(s)`$ as in §[4](04-decomposing-the-remainder-r-r1-r2.md#sec-decomp). Then $`\mathrm{Yin}(s)`$ and $`\mathrm{Yang}(s)`$ both lie on the line through the real point $`\hat d_1=d_1M^{\sigma}`$ with direction $`e^{i(2\omega+\psi)}`$:*

<a id="eq-yinyang-line"></a>

```math
\mathrm{Yin}=\hat d_1+\bigl(d_2M^{\sigma}\bigr)e^{i(2\omega+\psi)},
\qquad
\mathrm{Yang}=\hat d_1+\bigl(d_2M^{\sigma}-|\chi|M^{2\sigma-1}\bigr)
e^{i(2\omega+\psi)},\qquad\text{(39)}
```

*with real coefficients. In particular the line through the two points meets the real axis at the single point*

```math
\hat d_1\;=\;d_1M^{\sigma}\;=\;\frac{R_1}{M^{-s}},
```

*the fractional amount of Table [1](05-the-remainders-are-one-more-partial-summand.md#tab-frac): the ratio is real because R₁ lies along the next main summand $`M^{-s}`$, and it equals $`|R_1|/|M^{-s}|`$ whenever $`d_1>0`$.*



*Proof.* Since *M* is a positive real base, $`M^s=M^{\sigma}e^{i\omega}`$. Substituting the two-direction form [(21)](04-decomposing-the-remainder-r-r1-r2.md#eq-R-two-dirs) of the remainder, $`R=d_1e^{-i\omega}+d_2e^{i(\omega+\psi)}`$, into [(37)](#eq-yin1),

```math
\mathrm{Yin}
=R\,M^{\sigma}e^{i\omega}
=d_1M^{\sigma}+d_2M^{\sigma}e^{i(2\omega+\psi)}.
```

For the yang point, $`\chi M^{2s-1}=|\chi|M^{2\sigma-1}e^{i(2\omega+\psi)}`$, and subtracting it from $`\mathrm{Yin}`$ gives the second formula of [(39)](#eq-yinyang-line). The coefficients $`d_1M^{\sigma}`$, $`d_2M^{\sigma}`$, and $`d_2M^{\sigma}-|\chi|M^{2\sigma-1}`$ are real by Proposition [4.1](04-decomposing-the-remainder-r-r1-r2.md#prop-weights)(ii), and the direction $`e^{i(2\omega+\psi)}`$ is not horizontal exactly when $`\sin(2\omega+\psi)\neq0`$, so the line meets the real axis at the single point $`d_1M^{\sigma}=\hat d_1`$. ◻


Whenever $`0<d_2<|\chi|M^{\sigma-1}`$, that is, whenever the dual fractional amount is strictly between $`0`$ and $`1`$, the two coefficients in [(39)](#eq-yinyang-line) have opposite signs: the yin and yang points then lie on opposite sides of the real axis and the segment itself, not merely its extension, crosses at $`\hat d_1`$.

Nothing in the construction is tied to the critical line, and the curves converge to the limit curves of §[6.4](#sec-yinyang-inf) at every σ. Figure [7](#fig-yinyang-asym) draws the yin curve against the *reflected* yang curve $`1-\mathrm{Yang}(s)`$ over $`0\leq T\leq7`$ at three values of σ. The point reflection $`z\mapsto1-z`$ exchanges the two ends of the unit segment, and under it the yang family lands on top of the yin family; the two limit traces coincide exactly, since a direct computation from [(33)](#eq-yin-inf)–[(34)](#eq-yang-inf), using $`\Psi(1-x)=\Psi(x)`$, gives

<a id="eq-refl-yang"></a>

```math
1-\mathrm{Yang}_{\infty}(x)\;=\;\mathrm{Yin}_{\infty}(1-x).\qquad\text{(40)}
```

So a single dashed teardrop, the trace of $`\mathrm{Yin}_{\infty}`$, is the limit of both families.

The convergence visible in Figure [7](#fig-yinyang-asym) is a theorem, and the machinery used in Appendix [A](a-proof-of-proposition-4-1-reality-and-positivity.md#sec-positivity) already contains most of its proof: by [(37)](#eq-yin1) the yin point is *R* carried through the frame map [(36)](#eq-frame-map), and Appendix [A](a-proof-of-proposition-4-1-reality-and-positivity.md#sec-positivity) knows the size, the sign, and the phase of *R* to exactly the accuracy needed. The one genuinely new ingredient is a polar form of the limit curves.

<a id="lem-yinf-polar"></a>


**Lemma 6.4** (the two limit curves are really a single curve, described explicitly: $`\mathrm{Yang}_{\infty}(x)=\mathrm{Yin}_{\infty}(x-\tfrac12)`$). *Write $`A(x)=2\pi\bigl(x^2-\tfrac1{16}\bigr)`$ as in [(30)](#eq-F-C0) and $`\theta(x)=2\pi x(1-x)-\tfrac{3\pi}8`$, the phase of [(197)](a-proof-of-proposition-4-1-reality-and-positivity.md#eq-cosu). Then for all real x, with removable points filled in by continuity,*

<a id="eq-yinf-polar"></a>

```math
\mathrm{Yin}_{\infty}(x)\;=\;1-\Psi(x)\,e^{-iA(x)}
\;=\;\Psi\bigl(x+\tfrac12\bigr)\,e^{\,i\theta(x)},\qquad\text{(41)}
```

*and the yang limit is the yin limit half a unit behind in the argument:*

<a id="eq-yinf-halflag"></a>

```math
\mathrm{Yang}_{\infty}(x)
\;=\;\mathrm{Yin}_{\infty}\bigl(x-\tfrac12\bigr)
\;=\;\mathrm{Yin}_{\infty}(x)-e^{\,2i\theta(x)}.\qquad\text{(42)}
```



*Proof.* For [(41)](#eq-yinf-polar), multiply through by $`\cos(2\pi x)`$ and write $`\varphi=2\pi x`$. On the left, $`\Psi(x)\cos(2\pi x)=\cos(A-\varphi)`$, since $`2\pi(x^2-x-\tfrac1{16})=A(x)-\varphi`$. On the right, $`\cos\bigl(2\pi(x+\tfrac12)\bigr)=-\cos(2\pi x)`$ and $`(x+\tfrac12)^2-(x+\tfrac12)-\tfrac1{16}=x^2-\tfrac5{16}`$, so $`\Psi(x+\tfrac12)\cos(2\pi x)=-\cos\bigl(A(x)-\tfrac\pi2\bigr)=-\sin A`$; also $`\theta(x)=\varphi-A-\tfrac\pi2`$, so $`e^{i\theta}=-ie^{\,i(\varphi-A)}`$. The claim therefore reads

```math
\cos\varphi-\cos(A-\varphi)\,e^{-iA}
\;=\;(-\sin A)\,(-i)\,e^{\,i(\varphi-A)}
\;=\;i\sin A\,e^{\,i(\varphi-A)},
```

and expanding the left side settles it: the real part is $`\cos\varphi-\cos(A-\varphi)\cos A=\sin A\,\sin(A-\varphi)`$ (product formula), the imaginary part is $`\sin A\,\cos(A-\varphi)`$, and $`\sin A\bigl(\sin(A-\varphi)+i\cos(A-\varphi)\bigr)
=i\sin A\,e^{-i(A-\varphi)}`$, which is the right side.

For [(42)](#eq-yinf-halflag), the definition [(34)](#eq-yang-inf) and the first equality of [(41)](#eq-yinf-polar) give $`\mathrm{Yang}_{\infty}(x)=e^{\,2i\varphi}\bigl(1-\mathrm{Yin}_{\infty}(x)\bigr)
=\Psi(x)\,e^{\,i(2\varphi-A)}`$. Applying [(41)](#eq-yinf-polar) at $`x-\tfrac12`$ instead, $`\mathrm{Yin}_{\infty}(x-\tfrac12)=\Psi(x)\,e^{\,i\theta(x-\frac12)}`$, and $`\theta(x-\tfrac12)=2\pi(x-\tfrac12)(\tfrac32-x)-\tfrac{3\pi}8
\equiv2\varphi-A\pmod{2\pi}`$, so the first equality holds. For the second, $`e^{\,2i\theta}=-e^{\,2i(\varphi-A)}`$, and

```math
\begin{align*}
\mathrm{Yin}_{\infty}(x)-\mathrm{Yang}_{\infty}(x)
&=1-\Psi\bigl(e^{-iA}+e^{\,i(2\varphi-A)}\bigr)
=1-2\Psi\cos\varphi\,e^{\,i(\varphi-A)}\\
&=1-2\cos(A-\varphi)\,e^{\,i(\varphi-A)}
=-e^{\,2i(\varphi-A)}
=e^{\,2i\theta(x)},
\end{align*}
```

using $`\Psi\cos\varphi=\cos(A-\varphi)`$ in the middle step. ◻


<a id="thm-yin-convergence"></a>


**Theorem 6.5** (the Yin and Yang curves converge to the limit curves, $`\mathrm{Yin}_{\infty}`$ and $`\mathrm{Yang}_{\infty}`$). *Fix $`x\in(0,1)`$ and $`\sigma\in(0,1)`$. Then*

```math
\lim_{\substack{T\to\infty\\ \{T\}=x}}\mathrm{Yin}(s)
\;=\;\mathrm{Yin}_{\infty}(x),
\qquad
\lim_{\substack{T\to\infty\\ \{T\}=x}}\mathrm{Yang}(s)
\;=\;\mathrm{Yang}_{\infty}(x),
```

*at rate $`O(1/T)`$, uniformly for x and σ in compact subsets of $`(0,1)`$; the limit does not depend on σ.*


The proof needs only Gabcke’s bound [(201)](a-proof-of-proposition-4-1-reality-and-positivity.md#eq-gabcke) on the critical line, and off it additionally Siegel’s first-order asymptotics [(44)](#eq-R-general), the same input as in the proof of Proposition [4.1](04-decomposing-the-remainder-r-r1-r2.md#prop-weights)(iv).


*Proof.* Throughout, *C₀* and Ψ are the same function (§[6.4](#sec-yinyang-inf)).

*Step 1 (the yin point in asymptotic closed form).* By [(37)](#eq-yin1), $`\mathrm{Yin}=R\,M^s=R\,(m+1)^{\sigma}e^{\,i\omega}`$, with $`M=m+1`$ for non-integer *T*. On the critical line $`R=e^{-i\vartheta}r`$ with *r* real (Step 1 of the proof of Proposition [4.1](04-decomposing-the-remainder-r-r1-r2.md#prop-weights)(iii)), so $`\mathrm{Yin}=\sqrt{m+1}\;r\,e^{\,iu}`$, and inserting [(200)](a-proof-of-proposition-4-1-reality-and-positivity.md#eq-r-two-cases)–[(201)](a-proof-of-proposition-4-1-reality-and-positivity.md#eq-gabcke),

<a id="eq-yin-asymp"></a>

```math
\mathrm{Yin}
=\Bigl(\tfrac{m+1}{a}\Bigr)^{\!1/2}(-1)^{N-1}\bigl(C_0(\hat p)+E\bigr)
e^{\,iu}
\;+\;\bigl[N=m{+}1\bigr]\,\bigl(1+e^{\,2iu}\bigr),\qquad\text{(43)}
```

where the extra summand of [(200)](a-proof-of-proposition-4-1-reality-and-positivity.md#eq-r-two-cases) became the closed pair $`2\cos u\,e^{\,iu}=1+e^{\,2iu}`$. Off the line, the first term of Siegel’s asymptotic expansion of the remainder at fixed σ \[21\], the same input used in the proof of Proposition [4.1](04-decomposing-the-remainder-r-r1-r2.md#prop-weights)(iv), gives

<a id="eq-R-general"></a>

```math
\begin{split}
R(s)={}&(-1)^{N-1}\,\frac{C_0(\hat p)}{2}
\Bigl(a^{-\sigma}e^{-i\tilde\vartheta(t)}
+\chi(s)\,a^{\sigma-1}e^{+i\tilde\vartheta(t)}\Bigr)\\
&+\bigl[N=m{+}1\bigr]\bigl((m{+}1)^{-s}+\chi\,(m{+}1)^{s-1}\bigr)
\;+\;O_\sigma\bigl(a^{-\sigma-1}\bigr),
\end{split}\qquad\text{(44)}
```

with the elementary phase $`\tilde\vartheta(t)=\tfrac t2\log\tfrac t{2\pi}-\tfrac t2-\tfrac\pi8
=\vartheta(t)+O(1/t)`$. The error is additive because Siegel’s expansion advances in powers of $`a^{-1}`$, one step of which is all we keep. Against the displayed term it is a relative $`O(1/a)=O(1/T)`$, the order of Gabcke’s *E* in [(201)](a-proof-of-proposition-4-1-reality-and-positivity.md#eq-gabcke): the two pieces of the parenthesis align rather than cancel, since $`|\chi(s)|\,a^{\sigma-1}=a^{-\sigma}\bigl(1+O(1/t)\bigr)`$ and their phases differ by $`\arg\chi+2\tilde\vartheta=O_\sigma(1/t)`$, so the parenthesis has modulus $`2a^{-\sigma}\bigl(1+o(1)\bigr)`$, while *C₀* stays between $`\cos\tfrac{3\pi}{8}=0.3826\ldots`$ and $`\cos\tfrac\pi8=0.9238\ldots`$ on $`[0,1]`$. The frame factor $`(m+1)^{\sigma}e^{\,i\omega}`$ carries the two pieces of the parenthesis to $`\bigl(\tfrac{m+1}{a}\bigr)^{\sigma}e^{\,i(\omega-\tilde\vartheta)}`$ and $`|\chi|\,a^{\sigma-1}(m+1)^{\sigma}e^{\,i(\omega+\psi+\tilde\vartheta)}`$, and since $`|\chi(\sigma+it)|=a^{1-2\sigma}\bigl(1+O(1/t)\bigr)`$ (by Stirling’s formula) and $`\psi=-2\vartheta+O_\sigma(1/t)`$, both tend to $`e^{\,iu}`$; likewise the Iverson pair maps to $`1+|\chi|(m+1)^{2\sigma-1}e^{\,i(2\omega+\psi)}\to1+e^{\,2iu}`$. In every case

```math
\mathrm{Yin}=(-1)^{N-1}C_0(\hat p)\,e^{\,iu}\bigl(1+O_\sigma(1/T)\bigr)
+\bigl[N=m{+}1\bigr]\bigl(1+e^{\,2iu}\bigr)\bigl(1+O_\sigma(1/T)\bigr).
```

*Step 2 (the phase, kept as an angle).* The two case expansions of *u* displayed before [(197)](a-proof-of-proposition-4-1-reality-and-positivity.md#eq-cosu), kept as angles rather than used only through their cosines, combine with [(196)](a-proof-of-proposition-4-1-reality-and-positivity.md#eq-cut-cases) into the single statement

```math
u\;\equiv\;\pi(m+1)+\theta(x)+O(1/T)\pmod{2\pi},
```

while $`\hat p\to x+\tfrac12`$ (with $`N=m`$) for $`x<\tfrac12`$ and $`\hat p\to x-\tfrac12`$ (with $`N=m+1`$) for $`x>\tfrac12`$.

*Step 3 (case $`x<\tfrac12`$).* Here $`N=m`$, the Iverson term is absent, and the sign $`(-1)^{N-1}=(-1)^{m-1}`$ cancels against $`e^{\,i\pi(m+1)}=(-1)^{m+1}`$. With $`\tfrac{m+1}a\to1`$ (by [(195)](a-proof-of-proposition-4-1-reality-and-positivity.md#eq-a-vs-T)) and $`C_0=\Psi`$,

```math
\mathrm{Yin}\;\longrightarrow\;
\Psi\bigl(x+\tfrac12\bigr)e^{\,i\theta(x)}
\;=\;\mathrm{Yin}_{\infty}(x)
```

by the polar form [(41)](#eq-yinf-polar).

*Step 4 (case $`x>\tfrac12`$).* Here $`N=m+1`$, the sign product is $`-1`$, and the Iverson pair is present:

```math
\mathrm{Yin}\;\longrightarrow\;
1-\Psi\bigl(x-\tfrac12\bigr)e^{\,i\theta(x)}+e^{\,2i\theta(x)} .
```

Since $`\theta(x)=-A(x-\tfrac12)`$ exactly, the first two terms are $`\mathrm{Yin}_{\infty}(x-\tfrac12)`$, and the single-curve relation [(42)](#eq-yinf-halflag) finishes: $`\mathrm{Yin}\to\mathrm{Yin}_{\infty}(x-\tfrac12)+e^{\,2i\theta(x)}
=\mathrm{Yin}_{\infty}(x)`$.

*Step 5 (the yang point).* By [(38)](#eq-yang1), $`\mathrm{Yang}-\mathrm{Yin}=-\chi\,M^{2s-1}`$, whose modulus $`|\chi|(m+1)^{2\sigma-1}=\bigl(\tfrac{m+1}a\bigr)^{2\sigma-1}
\bigl(1+O(1/t)\bigr)\to1`$ and phase $`2\omega+\psi=2u+O_\sigma(1/t)\equiv2\theta(x)+O(1/T)`$, so the subtracted vector tends to $`e^{\,2i\theta(x)}`$ and $`\mathrm{Yang}\to\mathrm{Yin}_{\infty}(x)-e^{\,2i\theta(x)}
=\mathrm{Yang}_{\infty}(x)`$, again by [(42)](#eq-yinf-halflag).

Every ingredient contributes $`O(1/T)`$: the amplitude ratio $`\tfrac{m+1}a=1+O(1/T)`$, Gabcke’s $`E=O(1/a)`$, the phase remainder of Step 2, and off the line the $`O_\sigma(1/t)`$ terms of ψ and $`|\chi|`$ together with the $`O_\sigma(a^{-\sigma-1})`$ of [(44)](#eq-R-general), which against a leading term of size $`a^{-\sigma}`$ is again $`O(1/T)`$. At the seam $`x=\tfrac12`$ the two case formulas agree: $`\Psi(1)=\Psi(0)=\cos\tfrac\pi8`$ and $`1+e^{\,i\pi/4}=2\cos\tfrac\pi8\,e^{\,i\pi/8}`$, so both give $`\cos\tfrac\pi8\,e^{\,i\pi/8}`$, which is $`\mathrm{Yin}_{\infty}(\tfrac12)`$; the narrow boundary intervals of [(196)](a-proof-of-proposition-4-1-reality-and-positivity.md#eq-cut-cases) near $`x=0,1`$ are excluded by keeping *x* in a compact subset of $`(0,1)`$. ◻


Three attributes of the yin and yang curves stand out.

**1.** At any σ, the yin and yang curves approach $`\mathrm{Yin}_{\infty}`$ and $`\mathrm{Yang}_{\infty}`$ as $`T\to\infty`$: this is Theorem [6.5](#thm-yin-convergence), at rate $`O(1/T)`$, with a limit that does not depend on σ. Figure [7](#fig-yinyang-asym) shows the successive orbits settling onto the limit curves as $`T\to\infty`$ for each of $`\sigma=0.05`$, $`\tfrac12`$, and $`0.95`$. The chord joining the pair is a *unit needle*: its length is $`|\chi|M^{2\sigma-1}`$, exactly $`1`$ at every *T* on the critical line and $`1+O(1/T)`$ at any fixed σ. On the critical line, and at $`T\to\infty`$, the two curves travel at constant unit separation.

**2.** The curves are not symmetrical (when not at infinity), and this becomes obvious once the yang curve is reflected to $`1-\mathrm{Yang}(s)`$ and compared with the yin curve: by [(40)](#eq-refl-yang) the two limit traces coincide exactly, but at finite *T* the two families only interleave, closing on one another as *T* grows and coinciding at no finite *T*.[^4] The global asymmetry coexists with an instantaneous balance: on the critical line [(39)](#eq-yinyang-line) with $`d_1=d_2`$ makes the crossing point equidistant from $`0`$ and the yin point (both distances $`\hat d_1`$) and from $`1`$ and the yang point (both $`1-\hat d_1`$), so the pair rides two concentric circles centered at the moving crossing, with radii summing to $`1`$.

**3.** Which family runs outside depends on σ. For σ close to $`1`$ the yin curve runs inside the reflected yang curve; for σ close to $`0`$ it runs outside; and on the critical line the two swap sides near $`\{T\}=\tfrac12`$, one crossing outward as the other crosses inward: at matched positions on the teardrop the two offsets from the limit trade signs. The swap is forced by an exact duality: since $`\chi(s)\chi(1-s)=1`$ exchanges the main and dual sums, $`R(1-s)=\chi(1-s)\,R(s)`$, and with $`\lambda=\chi(1-s)M^{1-2s}`$,

```math
\mathrm{Yin}(1-s)=\lambda\,\mathrm{Yin}(s),\qquad
\mathrm{Yang}(1-s)=\lambda\bigl(\mathrm{Yin}(s)-1\bigr),\qquad
\lambda\,\bigl(\mathrm{Yin}-\mathrm{Yang}\bigr)(s)=1,
```

so reflecting $`\sigma\mapsto1-\sigma`$ (at conjugate height) interchanges yin and yang and swaps the needle with the unit segment.

The nearest fixed map, the time-reversed point reflection $`\mathrm{Yang}(T)\approx1-\mathrm{Yin}(2m{+}1{-}T)`$ at the same σ, becomes exact only as $`T\to\infty`$.
